# Brand Voice

This is the source of truth for the brand voice of Kilimanjaro True Venture. Read this before writing or editing any user-facing copy on the site. It is intentionally short and opinionated.

## Voice in one sentence

Kilimanjaro True Venture sounds like a Tanzanian outdoor co-op: locally rooted, technically credible, quietly confident, never macho.

## Who we write for

Most visitors are first-time travelers researching Tanzania for the first time. They've heard of Kilimanjaro and safari. They probably don't know Machame from Lemosho. They're comparing 5 to 10 operators. They are skeptical of marketing.

Write for them. Don't assume route knowledge. Don't explain things they already know (like what a safari is). When in doubt, write for the smart, careful, undecided traveler. Not the experienced one.

## The takeaway

If a stranger remembers one thing after 90 seconds on the site, it should be:

> **Tanzanian-owned and Tanzanian-guided. The legitimate one.**

Every page should reinforce this somewhere. When there's a choice between a generic claim ("we have great guides") and the specific one ("our guides are Tanzanian and have been working these mountains for years"), pick the specific one.

## What we sound like

- **Earned, not asserted.** Confidence comes from specifics: kilometers, altitude, route names, years. Not from adjectives.
- **Direct.** Short sentences. Adjectives earn their place.
- **Grounded.** We talk about the mountain, the porter, the route, the season. Not "the magic of Africa."
- **Quietly skeptical of marketing language.** If a sentence could appear verbatim on any other tour operator's site, it is wrong.
- **Communitarian.** Plural for operations ("our guides", "we"). First-person for Abu's voice on the About page only. The brand is a co-op of locals, not a corporation.
- **Patient with the reader.** Most visitors do not know what they are doing yet. Do not punish that. Explain what needs explaining without being condescending.

**Reference voices:** Patagonia (earned, environmental, slightly anti-corporate) and REI (informative, communitarian, technically credible). The site should feel like one of those companies happened to be Tanzanian and happened to run treks.

## What we do NOT sound like

The voice we are actively avoiding is **adventure-bro**.

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Crush the summit" | "Reach the summit" |
| "Push your limits" | "Test yourself" or describe the trek concretely |
| "Only legends" / "Are you ready?" | Cut entirely. No rhetorical questions. |
| "Epic", "ultimate", "insane" | Specific words. "Six-day trek." "5,895m peak." |
| "Dominate" / "Conquer" | "Climb." "Summit." (Hero title "Conquer the Roof of Africa" is grandfathered for now.) |
| "Adventure of a lifetime" | Cut. Cliché. |

We are also avoiding:

- **Corporate luxury.** "Embark on a transformative journey..."
- **Budget aggregator.** "Cheapest Kili treks! Save 40%!"
- **Generic SEO travel.** "Discover the magic of Tanzania."
- **Charity-first framing at top of funnel.** Abu Hope Foundation matters and has its own page. It does not lead the home page.

## Words and phrases

**Keep**
- "Tanzanian" / "Tanzanian-owned" / "Tanzanian-guided"
- "Porter" (used factually and respectfully)
- Specific place names (Machame, Lemosho, Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Tarangire)
- Specific numbers (60km, 5,895m, 6 days)
- Abu's name (only on About page and contexts where the visitor has already met him)

**Cut on sight**
- "embark", "journey of a lifetime", "transformative"
- "magic", "magical"
- "crush", "dominate", "conquer" (body copy)
- "authentic" (has been used to mean nothing)
- "professionally guided" (every operator says this)
- "unforgettable"

## Mechanical rules (mirrored from CLAUDE.md)

- No em dashes. Use periods or semicolons.
- No Oxford commas.
- No "not just X, but Y" constructions.
- No setup language ("In conclusion", "That said").
- No vague metaphors.

## Operational proof topics

"Earned not asserted" only works if the writing has substance to earn from. Below are the SME topics that *are* the proof of expertise for a Tanzania trekking operator. **A tour page that doesn't surface most of these is generic. The copy can be voice-perfect and still fail the trust test.** Use this list when writing or editing any tour page, especially Kilimanjaro routes. Calibrate by page type per the Per-Page Depth Strategy in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Safety & altitude**
- Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS): symptoms, why it happens, your protocol
- Pulse oximetry: when and how often you check
- "Climb high, sleep low": named acclimatization principle
- Evacuation procedure: who decides, how, by what vehicle, to where
- Oxygen carried per expedition (number of bottles, who's trained on it)
- Mortality on Kilimanjaro stated factually (~10/yr, 0.02%)

**Route & summit**
- Route success rate by day count (5-day vs 7-day vs 8-day)
- Daily itinerary with altitude gained, kilometers, hours walking
- Why a longer route is safer (acclimatization, not endurance)
- Comparative route notes: scenery, traffic, gradient

**Porter & crew ethics**
- KPAP partner status with link to IMEC partner list
- Porter wage stance (specific number above industry minimum)
- Load limit policy (kg per porter)
- Crew-to-client ratio
- Named lead guides with summits-led count, languages, certifications (WFR where applicable)

**What's included (line-item, not bundled adjective)**
- Park fees (broken out; they're ~35% of cost)
- Tents, sleeping mats, mess tent, toilet tent
- Oxygen, pulse oximeter, first-aid kit
- Food (specific; "three hot meals a day with hot drinks")
- Porter wages and tips guidance
- Transport to and from gate
- Pre and post-climb hotel nights

**Practical**
- Best months to climb (by route; northern routes get less rain)
- Deposit, postponement, refund policy
- Training guidance
- Packing list (or link to one)
- Insurance partner (named; e.g., Global Rescue)

**Geographic & cultural**
- Specific place names (Machame Gate, Barranco Wall, Stella Point, Uhuru Peak)
- Local context an outsider couldn't write: which villages crew come from, which markets supply food, which years the routes shifted

If a tour page has fewer than ~60% of the topics relevant to it, the page is too thin, regardless of how good the voice is. Voice without substance is decoration.

## A test before publishing

Before any copy ships:

1. Could a competitor write this verbatim? If yes, rewrite.
2. Does this sound like Patagonia or REI happened to be Tanzanian? If no, rewrite.
3. Is there a specific number, place, name, or fact in this paragraph? If no, consider adding one.
4. Is there an adjective that has not earned its place? Cut it.
